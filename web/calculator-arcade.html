Getting the most out of this calculator

A quick guide to the buttons above, how the math is evaluated, and a few habits that make everyday calculations faster and less error-prone.

The basics

Tap the number keys to enter a value, then a symbol — +, −, ×, or ÷ — followed by another number. Press = to see the result.

  • C clears everything and starts over from zero.
  • ⌫ deletes just the last digit you typed, so a typo doesn't cost you the whole entry.
  • % divides the number on screen by 100 — handy for tips, discounts, and tax.
  • ± flips the sign of the number on screen.
  • √ and x² apply straight away to whatever is on screen, without waiting for =.
  • . adds a decimal point; it only takes effect once per number.

The memory keys

The four keys along the top row hold a single stored number, which survives everything else you do — clearing the display doesn't touch it. A small M appears in the corner of the screen whenever something is stored.

  • M+ adds what's on screen to the stored number.
  • M− subtracts what's on screen from it.
  • MR recalls the stored number onto the display.
  • MC clears it back to zero.

This is the fastest way to total a column of numbers that each need their own little calculation first: work out each line, press M+, then read the total with MR at the end.

The tape

Every completed calculation is logged underneath the keypad, newest first, the way a printing calculator would spool it out. Tap any line to drop that result back onto the display and keep working from it. Clear wipes the list. Nothing is saved anywhere — the tape lives only in this page, and reloading empties it.

How the order of operations works here

This calculator evaluates left to right, the same way most simple pocket calculators do. That means it does not follow the "multiplication before addition" rule (PEMDAS) you may have learned in school for written expressions.

For example, entering 2 + 3 × 4 gives 20, not 14 — because it adds 2 and 3 first (making 5), then multiplies by 4. If you need multiplication or division to happen before an addition or subtraction, calculate that part first and carry the result into the next step by hand.

A few habits worth building

  1. Glance at the small number above the main display — it shows the value and operator you're carrying into the next step, so you can catch mistakes before pressing =.
  2. Use ⌫ instead of C when you only mistyped the last digit; it saves you from re-entering the whole calculation.
  3. Break long calculations into smaller steps and jot down intermediate results if the order of operations matters for what you're solving.
  4. For percentages of a number rather than "divide by 100," multiply first: e.g. for 15% of 80, enter 80 × 15 %.
  5. Check the tape before you trust a total — it's much easier to spot a wrong line there than to re-key the whole thing.

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn't this calculator do PEMDAS?
Basic calculators evaluate one operation at a time, in the order you press the keys, rather than parsing a full expression. Scientific and graphing calculators, and most spreadsheet software, do follow full operator precedence.
Can I chain several operations in a row?
Yes — each time you press a new operator, the previous step is calculated automatically and carried forward, so you can keep adding steps without pressing = in between.
What happens if I divide by zero?
The display will show Error, since division by zero isn't defined. Press C to clear it and start again.
Does the square root key work on negative numbers?
No — it shows Error, because a negative number has no real square root. Flip the sign with ± first if you meant to take the root of the positive value.
Does this calculator save my history?
No — nothing is stored. Refreshing the page or navigating away clears whatever was on screen, along with the tape and the memory key.
